# NBA Player Props Framework — V2.1

**Consolidated Governing Reference | 2026–27 NBA Season**

**Effective:** November 2, 2026  
**Status:** Governing framework  
**Supersedes:** V2.0 and earlier versions

---

# I. OBJECTIVE AND FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES

Identify and rank NBA player props supported by realistic statistical opportunities, sustainable player production, game-specific context and favorable market thresholds.

**Foundational principle: External models project. We independently validate, evaluate and rank.**

The framework must:

1. Evaluate both offenses and defenses before identifying player-specific selections.
2. Establish how the game environment creates and distributes statistical opportunities.
3. Distinguish opportunity volume, opportunity quality and conversion efficiency.
4. Reconstruct playing time and statistical responsibilities using current personnel evidence.
5. Screen every credible rotation player across all eight eligible statistical markets.
6. Preserve external projections rather than silently modifying or replacing them.
7. Separate model disagreements from independent sustainability assessments.
8. Validate individual statistical pathways using consistent evidence standards.
9. Establish independent statistical outlooks before evaluating market thresholds.
10. Evaluate Higher and Lower directions without predetermined bias.
11. Select the strongest supported market for each player.
12. Rank candidates using both basketball evidence and exact market terms.
13. Challenge finalist selections against excluded alternatives.
14. Preserve original pregame records for postgame evaluation.
15. Prioritize material evidence over unnecessary research complexity.

A favorable external projection identifies a candidate for investigation. It does not independently establish a validated selection.

Do not impose arbitrary projection margins, manufacture probabilities, introduce automatic exclusion rules or force selections to complete an entry.

# II. RESEARCH SOURCES AND DATA INTEGRITY

## 2.1 Primary sources and responsibilities

### Drop Coverage

- Game-specific player projections.
- Projected playing time.
- Available uncertainty estimates and projected ranges.
- Screening across all eight eligible markets.
- Personnel and matchup assumptions already incorporated into forecasts, where identifiable.

**Responsibility:** External game-specific projection baseline.

### DARKO

- Sustainable player production benchmarks.
- Per-minute and per-100 production expectations.
- Expected playing time and longer-term performance indicators.
- Evaluation of sustainable opportunity and conversion rates.
- Context for identifying unusually high or low recent production.

**Responsibility:** Sustainable production benchmarking, not automatic replacement of game-specific forecasts.

### NBA.com / Official NBA

- Official injury reports and player availability.
- Box scores, game logs and recent minutes.
- Field-goal attempts, free throws, three-point attempts and player statistics.
- Starting lineups and relevant team statistics.
- Available shot-location, shot-quality and playtype information.

**Responsibility:** Verified personnel, recent production and underlying basketball opportunities.

### Hoops Junkie

- Potential assists and passing opportunities.
- Rebound chances and collection rates.
- Available shot-attempt and three-point opportunity data.
- Relevant play-context information.

**Responsibility:** Independent opportunity validation.

### Databallr

- Offensive and defensive efficiency.
- Team Six Factors and possession characteristics.
- Shot distribution and scoring opportunities.
- Pace, rebounding, turnovers and matchup resistance.
- Supplementary zTS, historical Six-Factor RAPM and contextual metrics when useful.

**Responsibility:** Team environment and matchup analysis.

### Hashtag Basketball / RotoWire

- Projected and confirmed rotations.
- Expected and confirmed starters.
- Recent playing-time patterns.
- Personnel-dependent roles and lineup information.
- Available on/off and rotation-specific evidence.

**Responsibility:** Playing-time and personnel validation.

### NBA Team Profiles

- Established offensive and defensive tendencies.
- Statistical scouting context.
- Structural personnel changes.
- Historical matchup characteristics.

**Responsibility:** Supporting context, not a replacement for current statistics or game-day verification.

### KEEL / Other Verified Providers

- Current and historical game logs.
- Player statistics and production.
- Contextual evidence and corroborating data.
- Postgame statistical verification.

**Responsibility:** Historical production, independent corroboration and outcome review.

### Markets

- Actual PrizePicks lines.
- Verified sportsbook thresholds and prices.
- Applicable alternative lines.
- Goblin/Demon tiers and payout modifiers where relevant.

**Responsibility:** Exact market evaluation.

## 2.2 Source discipline

1. Use the newest relevant and compatible information.
2. Preserve source dates, denominators, sample sizes and statistical definitions.
3. Identify when evidence comes from different reporting windows.
4. Never treat overlapping games, related model outputs or on/off samples as independent confirmation.
5. Distinguish directly verified statistics from analytical inference.
6. Do not invent unavailable statistics, player assignments, opportunities or market thresholds.
7. Use verified alternative sources when primary information is inaccessible.
8. Investigate missing evidence when it can materially change an evaluation.
9. Recognize that historical data may not represent current personnel or roles.
10. Preserve external model projections exactly as published.

**Excluded sources:** PBP Stats and the BoxScore Lab CSV must not be used for player-prop research.

# III. SHARED ANALYTICAL MODEL

## 3.1 Volume — Quality — Conversion

Player production should be evaluated through three connected analytical channels.

### A. Opportunity volume

How many statistical opportunities should the player receive?

Evaluate playing time, responsibilities, possessions, attempts, chances and relevant opportunity ownership.

### B. Opportunity quality

How favorable are those opportunities?

Evaluate shot difficulty, creation context, teammate finishing opportunities, rebound positioning and matchup-specific resistance.

### C. Conversion efficiency

How sustainably does the player turn opportunities into statistical production?

Evaluate shooting accuracy, potential-assist conversion, rebound collection and other relevant outcomes relative to appropriate player-specific benchmarks.

**These channels are analytically distinct but not necessarily statistically independent.**

Improved opportunity quality may explain improved conversion. Do not apply separate numerical boosts or penalties for the same underlying change.

## 3.2 Market-specific application

### Points (PTS)

**Volume**
- FGA and FTA.
- Minutes and usage.
- Shot ownership.
- Offensive possessions and teammate competition.

**Quality**
- Shot locations.
- Assisted versus self-created attempts.
- Playtype and shot difficulty.
- Defensive coverage and resistance.
- Available shot-quality evidence.

**Conversion**
- Sustainable two-point and three-point efficiency.
- Free-throw conversion.
- Historical player-specific shooting benchmarks.
- Recent efficiency relative to shot mix.

### Three-pointers made (3PM)

**Volume**
- Three-point attempts.
- Attempts per minute.
- Playing time.
- Perimeter shot ownership.

**Quality**
- Catch-and-shoot versus off-dribble opportunities.
- Shot creation and available open-shot evidence.
- Defensive coverage.
- Teammate creation responsibilities.

**Conversion**
- Sustainable three-point accuracy.
- Historical shot-type efficiency when available.
- Recent accuracy relative to established ability and shot quality.

### Assists (AST)

**Volume**
- Potential assists.
- Passing responsibilities.
- Creation opportunities.
- Minutes and teammate shot opportunities.

**Quality**
- Recipient shot locations and shot quality.
- Defensive resistance.
- Offensive structure and teammate shot selection.

**Conversion**
- Teammate finishing relative to sustainable expectations.
- Potential-assist-to-assist conversion.
- Changes in teammate personnel or scoring ability.

### Rebounds (REB)

**Volume**
- Offensive and defensive rebound chances.
- Available missed shots.
- Minutes and rebounding responsibilities.
- Team and opponent shot volume.

**Quality**
- Shot locations and expected rebound positioning.
- Rebound distance and location where available.
- Teammate competition.
- Opponent personnel and rebounding structure.

**Conversion**
- Individual rebound-chance collection.
- Sustainable collection rates.
- Personnel-dependent collection changes.

### Combined markets

Eligible combinations:

- Points + rebounds (PR).
- Points + assists (PA).
- Rebounds + assists (RA).
- Points + rebounds + assists (PRA).

Evaluate each material component through its own volume, quality and conversion pathway.

Account for relationships between components.

Do not assume component outcomes are independent, double-count the same possession or automatically add separate benefits from one personnel change.

## 3.3 Sustainable capability versus game-specific allocation

Distinguish:

**Sustainable capability:** What the player can reasonably produce given an established role, opportunity rate and conversion environment.

**Game-specific allocation:** How many minutes, attempts, chances and responsibilities the player should receive in the current matchup.

A replacement player does not automatically inherit an absent teammate's usage, passing responsibilities or rebounding production.

A player may receive more minutes without receiving a proportional increase in every statistical opportunity.

# STAGE 1 — GAME ENVIRONMENT AND PERSONNEL

## Objective

Establish how both teams generate, prevent and distribute statistical opportunities under the expected personnel and game conditions.

## 1.1 Evaluate both offenses and defenses

Review relevant current evidence for:

- Offensive and defensive efficiency.
- Pace and expected possessions.
- Shot volume and distribution.
- Rim, midrange and three-point opportunities.
- Shot quality and sustainable finishing.
- Offensive and defensive Six Factors.
- Rebounding and missed-shot opportunities.
- Passing and assist creation.
- Free-throw generation and defensive fouling.
- Turnovers and possession sustainability.
- Transition and half-court characteristics.
- Meaningful recent scheme or performance changes.

Evaluate each offense against its opposing defense.

Do not infer that a fast game necessarily creates efficient scoring or that a favorable defensive statistic automatically benefits every opposing player.

## 1.2 Identify statistical opportunities and defensive resistance

Use three connected questions.

### Access

Can the offense realistically create the relevant statistical opportunities against this defense?

Consider attempt frequency, possession channels, available misses, passing opportunities, playing style and defensive scheme.

### Resistance

Does the defense materially change opportunity volume, opportunity quality or conversion expectations?

Review relevant:

- Shot-location frequencies.
- Defensive shot suppression.
- Playtype distributions.
- Defensive coverage.
- Turnover pressure.
- Rebounding resistance.
- Shot-clock tendencies.
- Current personnel.

### Ownership

Which players are positioned to receive and exploit those opportunities?

Evaluate individual offensive roles, shot ownership, creation responsibilities, rebounding assignments and teammate competition.

Distinguish:

**Defensive tendency:** A statistical opportunity or possession type commonly allowed.

**Exploitable vulnerability:** A demonstrated weakness that the opposing offense and relevant player can realistically exploit.

For scoring markets, distinguish shot access from shot quality and conversion efficiency.

When matchup uncertainty is material, investigate whether the defense actually disrupts the player's established scoring opportunities rather than relying solely on opponent shooting percentages.

High shot volume allowed does not necessarily establish favorable shot quality.

Poor opponent shooting percentages do not automatically demonstrate sustainable defensive resistance.

Positional allowances and aggregate team statistics are supporting context, not standalone proof of individual opportunity.

## 1.3 Personnel, rotations and responsibility redistribution

Verify:

- Official active/inactive designations.
- Expected and confirmed starters.
- Recent minutes and rotation patterns.
- Returning and replacement players.
- Playing-time restrictions.
- Foul and conditioning considerations.
- Teammate competition.
- Relevant personnel-specific statistics.
- Material scheme or role changes.

When personnel materially differs from the expected rotation:

1. Identify the actual personnel change.
2. Reconstruct plausible playing time from recent games and relevant personnel-specific evidence.
3. Determine how shots, passing responsibilities, rebound chances and other opportunities may be redistributed.
4. Distinguish genuine opportunity gains from superficial lineup changes.
5. Evaluate whether the change affects opportunity volume, quality or conversion.
6. Reassess the matchup and relevant game-script assumptions.
7. Identify whether the new evidence materially changes the independent outlook or confidence.

Prefer personnel-specific evidence when it provides meaningful information beyond broad team averages.

Use WOWY and lineup data with appropriate sample-size and overlapping-sample caution.

### Historical Six-Factor RAPM

When personnel changes materially affect the matchup, use historical Six-Factor RAPM where available and useful to identify whether a missing or returning player's influence primarily involves:

- Shooting efficiency.
- Turnover control or creation.
- Offensive or defensive rebounding.

Validate implications against current rotations and team statistics before modifying independent expectations.

Six-Factor RAPM estimates team-impact channels. It does not directly determine another player's box-score production.

Do not convert impact estimates into arbitrary prop adjustments.

Do not automatically modify Drop Coverage projections because personnel changes have occurred; the model may already incorporate those changes.

## 1.4 Establish realistic game scripts

Evaluate credible scenarios involving:

- Competitive normal rotations.
- Faster or slower possession environments.
- Changes in offensive efficiency.
- Matchup-driven opportunities.
- Foul trouble or playing-time restrictions.
- Potential noncompetitive fourth-quarter minutes.
- Material personnel-dependent adjustments.

Do not impose automatic blowout penalties, unsupported pace changes or speculative playing-time deductions.

**Stage 1 output:** Verified personnel, plausible rotations, both teams' environments, primary statistical opportunities, defensive resistance, opportunity ownership and material uncertainties.

# STAGE 2 — PROJECTIONS AND SUSTAINABILITY

## Objective

Screen the entire credible player pool, preserve external forecasts, diagnose disagreements and investigate sustainable production.

Research depth should increase as candidates become more credible.

## 2.1 Broad player and market screening

For every player with a credible rotation role, screen:

1. PTS.
2. REB.
3. AST.
4. 3PM.
5. PR.
6. PA.
7. RA.
8. PRA.

Exclude steals, blocks and stocks.

Consider Higher and Lower possibilities.

Do not prematurely exclude:

- Bench players with credible opportunities.
- Players experiencing sustainable role changes.
- Lower candidates.
- Combined markets.
- Alternative statistical lanes.
- Candidates with modest projection differences but meaningful independent opportunity evidence.

The broad screen identifies candidates for further investigation. It does not require exhaustive individual research on every rotation player.

## 2.2 Record external projections

Preserve where available:

**Drop Coverage**
- Projected minutes.
- Projected statistics across eligible markets.
- Uncertainty and expected ranges.
- Relevant identifiable assumptions.

**DARKO**
- Expected minutes.
- Sustainable individual production benchmarks.
- Per-minute or per-100 rates.
- Relevant long-term efficiency indicators.

**Markets**
- Actual verified thresholds when available.

Do not modify or silently replace published external forecasts.

## 2.3 Diagnose model disagreements

Model disagreement and production sustainability are separate questions.

Investigate four possible sources of disagreement:

### A. Minutes disagreement

Different expected playing time.

### B. Opportunity-volume disagreement

Different expectations for attempts, chances, possessions or responsibilities per minute.

### C. Opportunity-quality disagreement

Different assumptions about shot difficulty, recipient shot quality, rebound positioning or matchup conditions.

### D. Conversion disagreement

Different expectations for shooting, teammate finishing, rebound collection or other success rates.

**Important:** External models may not disclose the internal assumptions required to identify every disagreement.

Do not infer hidden model assumptions from differing projected totals alone.

Label the cause unresolved when independent evidence cannot establish it.

### Rate normalization

When appropriate, normalize DARKO's production rate to Drop's projected minutes as a diagnostic comparison.

Clearly label the result:

**Rate-normalized DARKO benchmark — not an official DARKO projection.**

Do not assume all production scales perfectly with playing time.

Changes in role, fatigue, usage and personnel can alter production rates.

## 2.4 Evaluate sustainability independently

Determine whether observed production reflects:

- Sustainable player ability.
- Structural opportunity changes.
- Personnel-driven responsibility changes.
- Changes in opportunity quality.
- Unusually favorable or unfavorable conversion.
- Small-sample variation.
- Matchup-dependent conditions.

Use historical stability, sample size, role continuity and player-specific benchmarks to assess evidence.

Do not automatically regress established players toward league average when better individual baselines exist.

### Three regression channels

**Opportunity-volume regression**

Investigate whether attempts, chances, touches, minutes or statistical responsibilities are likely to increase, remain stable or decrease.

**Opportunity-quality regression**

Investigate whether recent shot quality, teammate finishing opportunities or rebound positioning are likely to persist under the expected rotation and matchup.

**Conversion-efficiency regression**

Investigate whether actual statistical conversion is sustainable relative to player-specific ability and opportunity quality.

Identify causal overlap before assigning separate effects.

Do not double-count closely related explanations.

Recent overperformance is a reason to investigate sustainability, not automatically downgrade a player.

## 2.5 Focus the candidate pool

After the broad screen, prioritize candidates with credible statistical pathways or meaningful questions that can be resolved through independent evidence.

Candidates advance because their opportunities warrant investigation, not solely because models agree or recent hit rates are favorable.

**Stage 2 output:** Broad eight-market screen, serious candidate pool, preserved external forecasts, diagnosed or unresolved disagreements, and independent sustainability findings.

# STAGE 3 — INDEPENDENT BASKETBALL VALIDATION

## Objective

Determine whether each serious candidate has a credible basketball pathway independent of external model agreement.

Apply the established four-factor, eight-point rubric consistently.

## Factor 1 — Minutes and role (0–2)

**2 points:** Playing time and statistical responsibilities are independently established or strongly supported by relevant recent and personnel-specific evidence.

**1 point:** A credible role exists, but minutes or responsibility ownership remain materially uncertain.

**0 points:** The proposed workload or role lacks sufficient independent support.

Consider:

- Actual recent minutes.
- Rotation competition.
- Personnel-specific changes.
- Expected game scripts.
- Established responsibilities.
- Returning or replacement players.

## Factor 2 — Statistical opportunities (0–2)

**2 points:** Independently verified opportunity volume and relevant opportunity quality sufficiently support the candidate's proposed direction.

**1 point:** Some independent evidence supports the direction, but a material question remains about volume, ownership, quality or conversion.

**0 points:** The proposed statistical pathway is unsupported or meaningfully contradicted.

### Evidence standard

Full opportunity credit cannot be earned through:

- Model agreement alone.
- Recent box-score averages alone.
- Historical hit rates alone.
- Overlapping samples presented as independent confirmation.

Validate the actual statistical channel.

**PTS:** FGA, FTA, scoring locations, shot ownership, quality, teammate competition and expected minutes.

**AST:** Potential assists, creation responsibilities, passing activity, recipient opportunities and competing facilitators.

**REB:** Rebound chances, available misses, collection rates, positioning, teammate competition and frontcourt rotation.

**3PM:** Three-point attempts, attempts per minute, shot type, quality, role and sustainable conversion.

**Combined markets:** Independently validate material components and account for shared opportunities.

## Factor 3 — Matchup (0–2)

**2 points:** Independently verified matchup-specific opportunity or resistance evidence strongly supports the direction.

**1 point:** Relevant matchup evidence exists, but the individual effect remains materially uncertain.

**0 points:** The claimed matchup advantage lacks support or matchup evidence materially opposes the direction.

Evaluate:

1. The offense's ability to create the relevant opportunities.
2. The defense's actual resistance.
3. The player's ownership of the exploitable statistical channel.

For scoring, distinguish access, quality and conversion.

For assists, evaluate creation opportunities and recipient finishing conditions.

For rebounds, evaluate available misses, positioning and rebounding competition.

For three-pointers, evaluate attempt opportunities, defensive coverage and shot quality.

Do not assume a defense's general tendency establishes an exploitable individual vulnerability.

### Prevent rubric double-counting

Opportunity quality may inform both statistical-opportunity validation and matchup evaluation, but the justification for each score must be distinct.

Statistical opportunities evaluate whether the player's underlying pathway is supported.

Matchup evaluates how the opponent affects that pathway.

Do not award extra credit merely because one observation appears in several related datasets.

## Factor 4 — Sustainability and regression (0–2)

**2 points:** The statistical pathway is sustainably supported, with meaningful volume, quality and conversion regression evaluated and relevant failure risks addressed.

**1 point:** The direction is plausible, but sustainable production or a material regression channel remains uncertain.

**0 points:** The case depends heavily on unsupported production, unusual conversion or an implausible opportunity assumption.

Evaluate:

- Player-specific historical baselines.
- Sample reliability.
- Role continuity.
- Personnel-specific conditions.
- Volume regression.
- Quality regression.
- Conversion regression.
- Relevant matchup sustainability.

Do not apply arbitrary stabilization thresholds or automatic penalties for recent overperformance.

## 3.1 Evidence score interpretation

| Score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 7–8 | Strong independent validation |
| 5–6 | Credible but incomplete validation |
| 3–4 | Material uncertainty |
| 0–2 | Insufficient validation |

**Critical distinction:** Evidence scores measure the quality of independent support. They do not automatically determine A–D market grades.

A 7/8 score does not automatically justify A-range confidence.

A 5/8 score does not automatically require rejection.

Actual thresholds, outcome variability and failure scenarios remain decisive.

## 3.2 Focused versus intensive validation

Use proportional research depth:

**Broad screen:** Identify credible players and lanes using external projections and rotation context.

**Focused validation:** Investigate serious candidates using independent opportunity, matchup and sustainability evidence.

**Finalist validation:** Apply the complete rubric, establish independent outlooks and evaluate exact thresholds.

All serious candidates must receive consistent four-factor evaluation.

Do not expend equivalent research effort on players who lack credible rotation roles or statistical pathways.

**Stage 3 output:** Independently validated opportunities, four-factor scores, matchup assessments, sustainability findings and unresolved material evidence gaps.

# STAGE 4 — INDEPENDENT STATISTICAL OUTLOOK

## Objective

Establish realistic statistical expectations before deciding whether a market threshold is attractive.

## 4.1 Construct an independent outlook

Use:

- Verified playing time and role.
- Independently supported statistical opportunities.
- Opportunity volume.
- Opportunity quality.
- Sustainable conversion efficiency.
- Relevant teammate competition.
- Personnel-specific evidence.
- Defensive resistance.
- Plausible game scripts.
- Appropriate regression considerations.

Determine:

1. Expected statistical neighborhood.
2. Plausible normal production range.
3. Preferred Higher or Lower direction, if supported.
4. Material uncertainty.
5. Realistic failure scenarios.

Do not modify Drop or DARKO projections to manufacture agreement with the independent outlook.

Do not invent precise statistical expectations when the available evidence supports only a broader range.

## 4.2 Separate three forms of uncertainty

### Evidence uncertainty

Uncertainty caused by unavailable, conflicting or insufficient information.

Examples:
- Missing rebound-chance data.
- Unresolved opponent defensive assignments.
- Conflicting rotation information.

Seek additional evidence when resolution could materially change the decision.

Otherwise document the limitation.

### Rotation uncertainty

Uncertainty about expected playing time or statistical responsibilities.

Examples:
- Uncertain starter minutes.
- Returning player restrictions.
- Unclear frontcourt allocation.

Evaluate plausible personnel and rotation scenarios.

Do not invent replacement model projections.

### Outcome variability

Normal game-to-game statistical variation even when role and opportunities are reasonably established.

Examples:
- Three-point shooting variation.
- Teammate finishing variation.
- Rebound availability and collection.
- Foul trouble.

Recognize that strong evidence does not eliminate statistical variability.

Likewise, a relatively stable statistical market can still have weak supporting evidence.

## 4.3 Evaluate Higher and Lower independently

Do not assume Higher solely because:

- A model projection exceeds the market.
- Recent averages exceed the threshold.
- A player has a favorable historical hit rate.

Do not assume Lower solely because:

- Recent production is poor.
- Shooting efficiency appears unsustainable.
- The opponent has a strong aggregate defensive ranking.

Evaluate whether expected opportunities and sustainable conversion support each direction.

## 4.4 Establish realistic failure scenarios

Every serious finalist requires at least one basketball-grounded failure scenario.

Examples:

- A scorer maintains attempts but faces lower-quality shots.
- A shooter receives the expected three-point volume but converts below expectation.
- A passer creates opportunities but recipients fail to convert.
- A rebounder plays expected minutes but receives fewer collectable chances.
- A returning teammate changes opportunity ownership.
- The expected frontcourt rotation differs from the projected allocation.

Failure scenarios inform uncertainty, market grading and ranking.

They do not automatically invalidate otherwise supported candidates.

## 4.5 Preserve independence from the market

Establish the statistical outlook before deciding whether a specific threshold is favorable.

Market lines may be recorded during screening but must not be used to reverse-engineer a preferred projection.

**Stage 4 output:** Independent expectations, plausible ranges, supported directions, uncertainty classifications and realistic failure scenarios.

# STAGE 5 — MARKET EVALUATION AND RANKING

## Objective

Determine which independently validated statistical pathways offer the strongest value at actual market thresholds.

## 5.1 Verify exact market terms

Record:

- Player.
- Statistical category.
- Higher or Lower direction.
- Exact threshold.
- Market provider.
- Verification timing.
- Applicable sportsbook price.
- PrizePicks payout tier where relevant.
- Available alternative thresholds.

Do not assume lines are interchangeable across providers.

A 6.0 threshold is not equivalent to 5.5.

Account for pushes where applicable under the provider's rules.

Consider Goblin/Demon modifiers and payout implications when available.

## 5.2 Evaluate the exact threshold

Compare:

1. Independent statistical outlook.
2. Drop Coverage projection.
3. DARKO sustainability benchmark.
4. Verified market threshold.
5. Expected statistical variability.
6. Relevant push rules.
7. Applicable price or payout structure.
8. Material failure scenarios.

A projection-to-line difference identifies possible value; it does not establish validated value.

Investigate material model disagreement rather than automatically selecting or rejecting a candidate.

Do not impose arbitrary minimum projection margins.

Do not manufacture win probabilities.

## 5.3 Select the strongest lane per player

Compare individual and combined markets.

Prioritize the lane with the strongest combination of:

- Independently verified statistical opportunities.
- Sustainable production.
- Relevant matchup fit.
- Favorable exact threshold.
- Manageable uncertainty.
- Realistic failure scenarios.
- Applicable market terms.

Do not select several correlated markets for the same player solely because external projections favor all of them.

Compare component markets and combinations without treating their production as independent.

## 5.4 Assign A–D market confidence

### A range — Exceptional

Strong independent validation, reliable playing time, sustainable opportunities, favorable matchup-specific evidence and a distinctly attractive exact threshold.

Material failure scenarios are understood and comparatively limited.

### B range — Credible

A supported statistical pathway and reasonable market threshold, with some meaningful uncertainty.

### C range — Speculative

A plausible direction but incomplete opportunity evidence, substantial variability, role uncertainty or limited threshold advantage.

### D — Pass

Insufficient support, unresolved material contradictions or an unattractive available threshold.

**Evidence score and market grade remain separate evaluations.**

A candidate's grade must reflect both the independent basketball evidence and the specific market.

## 5.5 Rank candidates across the slate

Rank serious candidates using:

1. Independently verified statistical opportunity.
2. Minutes and responsibility confidence.
3. Sustainable production.
4. Matchup-specific opportunity and resistance.
5. Exact threshold and market terms.
6. Outcome variability.
7. Material failure scenarios.

Do not require arbitrary statistical margins or fixed numbers of selections.

Do not invent differences between candidates when the available evidence cannot distinguish them reliably.

## 5.6 Binding finalist-versus-exclusion challenge

Integrate the exclusion challenge directly into final ranking.

Before locking, compare the weakest finalist against the strongest excluded alternative.

Evaluate both using the same standards:

- Statistical opportunity volume.
- Opportunity quality.
- Role security.
- Matchup resistance.
- Sustainability and conversion.
- Actual market thresholds.
- Expected variability.
- Realistic failure scenarios.
- Correlation and game-script compatibility.

**Binding rule:** If the excluded candidate has a stronger independently supported case at its actual threshold, replace the weaker finalist.

If the evidence does not establish a meaningful difference, document the uncertainty rather than inventing ranking precision.

## 5.7 Evaluate correlation and compatibility

Distinguish:

**Compatible pathways:** Two props can succeed within the same plausible game environment.

**Positive correlation:** Outcomes benefit from shared team production or statistical events.

**Negative correlation:** One outcome may reduce another player's opportunities.

**Direct contradiction:** Selections materially require incompatible assumptions about the same opportunity allocation or game script.

Positive correlation does not automatically justify exclusion.

Evaluate entry concentration risk without forcing artificial diversification.

Also distinguish:

**Teammate opportunity competition:** A projection-level question about who receives attempts, chances or responsibilities.

**Entry correlation:** A market-level question about how selected outcomes may move together.

Do not conflate the two.

## 5.8 Final pregame recheck

Before locking:

1. Reconfirm material injuries and availability.
2. Verify confirmed starting personnel.
3. Check meaningful rotation changes.
4. Reconcile opportunity ownership.
5. Review material new matchup information.
6. Reconfirm exact market thresholds and terms.
7. Verify the strongest lane for each player.
8. Complete the binding exclusion challenge.
9. Review correlation and contradictory assumptions.
10. Preserve the pregame record.

**Stage 5 output:** Ranked selections, exact thresholds, confidence grades, evidence scores, documented risks and strongest excluded alternatives.

# IV. RESEARCH EFFICIENCY AND MATERIALITY

Research should be proportional to the decision.

## 4.1 Three levels of investigation

**Broad screening**

Identify credible rotation players and potential lanes without conducting exhaustive research on every option.

**Focused validation**

Investigate independent opportunity evidence, matchup support and sustainability for serious candidates.

**Finalist audit**

Apply full validation, independent statistical outlooks, exact-market evaluation and binding ranking comparisons.

These are research-depth levels within the existing framework, not additional formal stages.

## 4.2 Conditional diagnostics

Use advanced or supplementary information when it can materially resolve uncertainty, including:

- zTS.
- Historical Six-Factor RAPM.
- Shot-clock distribution.
- Detailed playtype efficiency.
- Shot-quality information.
- WOWY statistics.
- Relevant personnel-specific splits.

These metrics are conditional diagnostic tools, not mandatory requirements for every game or player.

Do not assume additional metrics improve forecasting accuracy without demonstrated incremental value.

## 4.3 Materiality-based research stopping rule

Additional research is justified when it could:

1. Change the independent statistical outlook.
2. Resolve a material disagreement.
3. Identify a meaningful failure scenario.
4. Clarify role or opportunity ownership.
5. Change the preferred Higher/Lower direction.
6. Change confidence or ranking.
7. Distinguish competing finalists.

Conclude supplemental research when additional evidence is unlikely to materially change the decision.

Document unresolved material uncertainty rather than conducting research without a defined analytical purpose.

Do not confuse incomplete information with inadequate analysis.

A research stopping rule does not override mandatory personnel verification, serious-candidate validation or final market rechecks.

# V. POSTGAME REVIEW

## Objective

Evaluate original decisions using evidence that was available before the game, distinguish avoidable analytical errors from normal statistical variance and improve the framework through recurring findings.

## 5.1 Preserve the original pregame record

Never retroactively modify:

- Drop projections.
- DARKO benchmarks.
- Independent statistical outlooks.
- Market thresholds.
- Market prices or tiers.
- Evidence scores.
- Pregame confidence grades.
- Original personnel assumptions.
- Locked selections.
- Documented failure scenarios.

## 5.2 Compare expectations with actual outcomes

For each selection, review:

1. Projected versus actual minutes.
2. Projected versus actual production.
3. Expected versus actual opportunity volume.
4. Expected versus actual opportunity quality, where measurable.
5. Expected versus actual conversion.
6. Personnel and rotation assumptions.
7. Matchup expectations.
8. Whether the documented failure scenario occurred.
9. Whether relevant pregame evidence was overlooked.

## 5.3 Classify analytical outcomes

### Successful process

The selection was reasonably supported and its statistical pathway was appropriately evaluated, regardless of whether ordinary variance produced a win or loss.

### Opportunity-volume error

The analysis incorrectly estimated attempts, chances, touches or statistical responsibilities.

### Opportunity-quality error

The analysis misunderstood shot difficulty, recipient finishing opportunities, rebound positioning or defensive resistance.

### Conversion-assumption error

Sustainable finishing, shooting, assist conversion or rebound collection was materially misestimated.

### Minutes/rotation error

Playing-time or role assumptions were materially incorrect.

### Matchup error

The offense's ability to generate opportunities or the defense's ability to disrupt them was incorrectly evaluated.

### Lane-selection error

Another market for the same player was more strongly supported using available pregame evidence.

### Finalist-ranking error

A stronger independently validated candidate was excluded.

### Ordinary statistical variance

The anticipated opportunities and reasonable assumptions occurred, but the realized outcome varied within plausible game-to-game uncertainty.

Multiple factors may contribute to the same result.

Do not assign precise causes when detailed opportunity evidence is unavailable.

## 5.4 Review excluded alternatives

Evaluate the strongest excluded candidates using their original pregame evidence.

Distinguish:

- Genuinely avoidable selection mistakes.
- Winning alternatives that were not clearly superior beforehand.
- Ordinary outcome variance.
- Later information unavailable before tipoff.

Do not use hindsight-only outcomes to manufacture superior pregame rankings.

## 5.5 Framework maintenance

Track recurring patterns across multiple slates.

Investigate whether:

- Minutes expectations are systematically inaccurate.
- Opportunity ownership is misallocated.
- Matchup resistance is overestimated or underestimated.
- Opportunity quality is being misinterpreted.
- Conversion expectations show persistent bias.
- Market selection or finalist ranking consistently favors weaker candidates.
- Additional diagnostics demonstrate useful predictive value.

Change the framework when recurring evidence supports improvement, not because of isolated losses.

# VI. EXECUTION STANDARDS

1. Follow the existing five stages in order.
2. Evaluate both offenses and defenses.
3. Verify personnel and reconstruct relevant rotations.
4. Distinguish shot access, defensive resistance and individual opportunity ownership.
5. Screen the credible player pool across all eight eligible markets.
6. Preserve Drop and DARKO projections without modification.
7. Separate model disagreement from sustainability assessment.
8. Diagnose volume, quality and conversion using independent evidence.
9. Avoid counting related or overlapping evidence as independent confirmation.
10. Use personnel-specific information when materially relevant.
11. Apply the four-factor, eight-point rubric to serious candidates.
12. Do not award full opportunity credit solely from projections, averages or hit rates.
13. Establish independent expectations before market evaluation.
14. Preserve evidence, rotation and outcome uncertainty as distinct concepts.
15. Evaluate Higher and Lower directions independently.
16. Verify exact market thresholds, prices, tiers and push implications.
17. Identify the strongest lane for each player.
18. Rank across games without forced selections or invented probabilities.
19. Complete the binding finalist-versus-exclusion challenge.
20. Evaluate teammate competition and entry correlation separately.
21. Recheck material news, rotations and lines before locking.
22. Preserve the original pregame record for postgame review.
23. Apply materiality-based research depth and stopping rules.
24. Avoid unsupported numerical adjustments and unnecessary methodological complexity.

# VII. OPERATING SEQUENCE

**Stage 1 — Environment and Personnel**

Verify personnel → Evaluate both offenses and defenses → Reconstruct rotations → Identify access, resistance and ownership → Establish game scripts.

**Stage 2 — Projections and Sustainability**

Screen all eight markets → Record Drop → Benchmark DARKO → Diagnose model disagreements → Evaluate sustainable volume, quality and conversion → Prioritize candidates.

**Stage 3 — Independent Validation**

Verify minutes and role → Validate statistical opportunities → Evaluate matchup-specific resistance → Assess sustainability and regression → Apply eight-point rubric.

**Stage 4 — Independent Outlook**

Establish expected production → Determine realistic ranges → Evaluate Higher/Lower directions → Classify uncertainties → Identify failure scenarios.

**Stage 5 — Markets and Ranking**

Verify exact thresholds → Compare projections and independent outlook → Identify strongest lanes → Assign A–D grades → Rank candidates → Challenge exclusions → Audit correlation → Recheck and lock.

**Postgame**

Preserve original record → Verify actual opportunities and outcomes → Identify process errors versus variance → Review exclusions → Track recurring findings → Refine only when justified.

# FINAL GOVERNING PRINCIPLE

**External projections identify possibilities.**

**Opportunity volume establishes how much statistical production is available.**

**Opportunity quality establishes how favorable those opportunities are.**

**Sustainable conversion determines how realistically those opportunities become production.**

**Independent validation establishes credibility.**

**Actual market thresholds determine selection quality.**

**Disciplined ranking determines what gets selected.**

Confidence must be earned through verified basketball evidence and favorable market conditions—not model agreement, recent hit rates, excessive research or the desire to complete an entry.
